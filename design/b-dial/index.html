Epsilon Indi B

Five photos of the same small patch of sky, taken between April 2025 and October 2026. Turn the dial and watch the middle: one dot will not stay put. It is a .

Brown dwarf. A ball of gas too small to shine like a star. It glows only from leftover heat, so infrared cameras see it best.

This one is 12 light years away. It looks like it barely moves, but between the first and last photo it slid 6.5 .

Arcsecond. A tiny angle on the sky. The full Moon is about 1,800 arcseconds wide; one pixel of this camera covers 3.
