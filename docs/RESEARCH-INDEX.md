# Research index

One line per published research note and per family of experiment files: the question it answers and its main finding. All notes were written on 14 November 2026 and label each claim VERIFIED (read at the source or produced by a command that day) or UNVERIFIED. Note 10 is not part of the published set, so the numbering skips it.

Notes live in `docs/research/`; experiment scripts, logs, JSON results and figures live in `docs/research/experiments/` (figures in `experiments/img/`).

## Notes

| Note | Question | Main finding |
|---|---|---|
| [01 Challenge and hackathon](research/01-challenge-and-hackathon.md) | What exactly do the challenge, the rules and the judges require? | Demo of at most 30 seconds or 7 slides (longer is ineligible for global judging); public links, English, at least one NASA dataset, every library listed, SI use disclosed, nobody under 18 shown or heard; deadline Sunday 15 Nov 23:59 local time; five judging criteria (Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, Presentation). About 144 public repositories already target this challenge, so a plain blink-by-coordinates is not original. |
| [02 WiseView and prior art](research/02-wiseview-and-prior-art.md) | What does the reference tool for this task (WiseView, from Backyard Worlds) do, and what carries over to SPHEREx? | WiseView keeps its whole state in the URL hash and its key features are time-resolved epochs, difference imaging and a Gaia overlay. No SPHEREx blink viewer existed at the IRSA site. The method does not transfer unchanged: SPHEREx has a different wavelength in every image, so epochs must be grouped by wavelength first. |
| [03 SPHEREx mission and data](research/03-spherex-mission-and-data.md) | How does SPHEREx observe, and what is in each data product? | 6 detectors, 102 channels from 0.75 to 5.0 µm, 6.15 arcsec pixels, 113.58 s exposures, about 4 all-sky passes in two years; each exposure at one sky position has a different wavelength. Includes the release DOIs (QR2 is 10.26131/IRSA652, correcting an earlier source), the FLAGS table and known caveats. |
| [04 IRSA coverage probe](research/04-irsa-coverage-probe.md) | Which SPHEREx images cover the five targets, and how do the archive services behave? | The archive held 1,628,318 plane rows (to 5 Oct 2026). Per target: Barnard 363 images in 5 passes, Epsilon Indi 465 in 4, WISE 0855 258 in 4, RW Aurigae 335 in 3, R Draconis 2,493 in 3. A cold TAP query takes 10 to 45 s; `INTERSECTS` gives wrong counts, `CONTAINS(POINT, poly)` is right. |
| [05 Cutout and CORS probe](research/05-irsa-cutout-cors-probe.md) | Can a browser read SPHEREx pixels, and at what cost? | IRSA TAP, SIA, cutouts and files send no CORS headers; the HiPS tiles and the S3 bucket do. A cutout is about 4 MB and 10 to 21 s whatever its size, with HTTP 503 when busy. S3 allows `Range` requests from any origin. |
| [06 Targets and Planet X science](research/06-targets-and-planet-x-science.md) | What are the five targets, where are they now, and can SPHEREx see them? | The challenge's WISE 0855 coordinates are a 2010 position; in 2025 to 2026 it is 2.0 to 2.2 arcmin west. Stars move only 0.3 to 2.5 pixels over the whole baseline, so group frames by pass. R Draconis and Barnard's Star saturate; Epsilon Indi B is the cleanest. SPHEREx cannot detect Planet Nine, so the tool must not promise it. |
| [07 Web visualisation stack](research/07-web-viz-stack.md) | Which technology should the viewer use? | Ranked options: a static front end plus a small proxy for the IRSA search (best fit for a weekend), Aladin Lite as base map, a Python rendering backend, Firefly, Pyodide (rejected). Key risks to test first: per-frame WCS differences and the cutout 503 behaviour. |
| [08 Detectability experiment](research/08-detectability-experiment.md) | Can a person see Barnard's Star and WISE 0855 move in per-pass SPHEREx frames? | Yes for both, but only without the bad-pixel flag mask (it erases Barnard's saturated star). WISE 0855 is detected in D5 and D6 with per-pass S/N 8.7 to 13.9 and moves as predicted (7.5 arcsec west measured against 8.0 predicted); D4 is a contaminated null because a static star walks into the aperture. |
| [09 S3 range reader and remaining targets](research/09-s3-range-reader-and-remaining-targets.md) | Can pixels be read straight from S3, and what do Epsilon Indi B, R Draconis and RW Aurigae show? | Three range requests per exposure (538 KB, 2.5 s), bit-identical to IRSA cutouts, zero failures. ZODI can be skipped. Epsilon Indi B is clearly visible in all 93 exposures and its motion matches the prediction (6.39 against 6.37 arcsec). RW Aurigae brightens by 40 percent at matched wavelength; R Draconis variability is marginal and its saturated core needs a ring-profile rule chosen after looking. |
| [11 Repos for graphics and experience](research/11-repos-graphics-experience.md) | Which open-source libraries could improve the look and interaction? | A top-10 of MIT, ISC, Zlib or BSD packages (post-processing, charts, motion, tour, URL state, i18n, sharing, UI). Licence traps: Remotion (not OSI), Shepherd.js and Intro.js (AGPL), Stellarium Web Engine (AGPL). Includes a reference 3D sky tool read for ideas only (its data is not reusable) and an attribution checklist. |
| [12 Repos for science and physics](research/12-repos-science-physics.md) | Which libraries and catalogues cover the physics, and is a "repository of all laws of physics" needed? | No: the missions need about ten formulas, all in astropy and astronomy-engine. Useful finds: astronomy-engine for Earth's position in the browser, unWISE and Legacy Survey cutouts with open CORS for a 2010 background layer. OpenStax's glossary is CC BY-NC-SA, incompatible with an Apache-2.0 repository, so write our own. |
| [13 Parallax feasibility](research/13-parallax-distance-feasibility.md) | Can students measure distance and speed from SPHEREx data? | Epsilon Indi B: parallax 306 +- 28 mas (10.9 sigma) with Gaia's proper motion, 3.3 +- 0.3 pc against Gaia's 3.64 pc; 330 +- 70 mas (4.7 sigma, marginal) fitted from SPHEREx alone. Not measurable for WISE 0855 or Barnard. Uncorrected positions give a confident but 60 percent wrong answer because of a seasonal frame shift. |
| [14 Video and design tooling](research/14-video-and-design-tooling.md) | How do we make the 30-second demo and the app's look, with free, licence-safe tools? | Do not install the `brag` plugin (telemetry, unreviewed dependencies); Remotion is not recommended; the fallback pipeline with Playwright and ffmpeg needs no new tool and is what `video/` implements. A 9-shot storyboard, audio and caption options, and a proposed dark Astryx theme "Skyblink" with an infrared accent. |
